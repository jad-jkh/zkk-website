Research approach

Research consultant vs doing it yourself or with AI tools

AI tools have made it much easier to get a first draft of market research. The harder question is when a draft is enough and when it is worth paying someone to do the work.

This guide supports our market research packages and pricing service.

Published 13 October 2026 by ZKK Consulting. Updated 10 November 2026.

Starting point

What AI tools are good at

If you are weighing a research consultant against doing the work yourself or with AI tools, it helps to start with what the tools do well. They are fast at summarizing a topic, suggesting how a market might be segmented, drafting an outline, listing candidate competitors, and explaining unfamiliar terms. For a founder who needs to get oriented in an afternoon, that is real value.

Doing the research yourself has its own strengths. You know your product and customers better than anyone, you can keep the work private, and the effort teaches you the market in a way a report cannot.

The limits show up when the output has to be relied on by someone else, such as an investor, a partner, or a board.

Where it gets difficult

Where unverified output becomes a risk

AI tools can state a figure with confidence that has no source behind it, cite a study that does not exist, or blend a real number with an invented one. This is a known behavior of language models, and it means every number and claim has to be checked against an original source before it goes into a deck or a model.

Checking takes time, and it is easy to skip when the text reads well. A market size that cannot be traced to its inputs tends to fall apart in the first diligence question: where does this number come from? If the honest answer is that a chatbot produced it, the damage reaches the rest of your materials.

This is a risk of unverified output, and it can be managed. Teams that check each claim against a primary source and write down their assumptions can use AI tools safely.

DIY

When doing it yourself is fine

DIY is a sensible choice when the research is for your own thinking and nobody else will rely on it. Examples include early idea screening, preparing questions for customer interviews, learning the vocabulary of a new industry, or deciding whether a topic deserves more attention.

It also works when you have time, you are comfortable checking sources, and the stakes of an error are low. AI tools can speed up the early steps, as long as you treat the output as leads to verify rather than findings. If you want to try the method yourself, our guide on how to size a market for a new device shows the steps.

Consultant

When a consultant earns the fee

A consultant tends to earn the fee when the work will be used outside your team. Fundraising is the clearest case, because investors will test the numbers. Other cases include partner or acquirer discussions, a go or no-go decision on a significant build, and any situation where you need the sources and arithmetic to hold up under questioning.

It also helps when your time is the scarce resource, or when the topic needs specialist knowledge, such as regulated products or technical standards.

Combining

Using both

The two approaches combine well. Some founders do the first pass themselves to sharpen the question, then hand the question to a consultant, which can keep the scope and the fee smaller. Others use AI tools to draft internal summaries from a consultant's sourced report.

Questions

Common questions

Can I use AI tools for market research?

Yes, with care. They are useful for orientation, drafting, and finding leads. Every number and claim should be checked against an original source before you rely on it, especially in material that investors or partners will see.

What is the main risk of AI-generated market research?

Unverified output. Models can state figures with no source, cite studies that do not exist, or mix real and invented data. Without source checks, errors can reach your deck or model unnoticed.

How much does a research consultant cost?

It varies widely. ZKK packages are fixed price: Launchpad $999, Growth $1,999, and Comprehensive $3,499, with add-ons generally $250. Details are on the services page.

Does a consultant use AI tools too?

Many do. The difference to ask about is how claims are verified, whether sources are cited, and whether assumptions are written down so you can review them.
