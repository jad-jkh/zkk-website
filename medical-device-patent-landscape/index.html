Overview

Why device teams look at patents early

Medical device patent landscape research helps a device team see who is already filing around a technology before the design is fixed and before a fundraise. Device categories can be dense, and a founder who has not looked at the patent record may be surprised by how much has been filed near their idea.

ZKK's Patent Landscape Research is a standalone service at $1,349 for up to 15 identified patents. Both founders are engineers, so the technical documents are read in context. The service is business research. It does not include legal conclusions of any kind.

Typical questions teams bring include which companies hold the most filings near a concept, whether activity is recent or mostly older, and which technical approaches show up repeatedly. The report answers these descriptively, using public records, and shows its sources.

What we map

What the report maps

  • Competitor filing patterns, including which companies file in your area and where activity is concentrated.
  • A technical feature matrix comparing the identified patents side by side, with apparent overlaps and distinctions drawn from public information.
  • Ownership and status information, as shown in public records.
  • Business and market context that helps you read the patent picture alongside the competitive picture.
  • A written report with the patent list, sources, and a documented search approach.

The scope is defined with you during scoping, including the technology area and the competitors you care most about. If the area needs more than 15 patents, we quote a custom scope.

The matrix is the part many teams use most. It lets an engineer and a business lead look at the same table and agree on what each patent appears to describe, without anyone needing to read all of the documents first.

Boundaries

What does the report not determine?

We do not determine patentability, freedom to operate, infringement, or validity, and we do not provide legal advice or opinions. The report is meant to be taken to qualified patent counsel as organized background. Counsel makes the legal judgments.

The same line applies to regulatory topics. We research public FDA information and standards as described on our expertise page, but we do not issue regulatory opinions or decide classification or clearance outcomes.

Device development is expensive, and discovering a crowded patent area late is costly. Looking early does not remove that risk, but it gives the team time to talk with counsel while the design can still change.
