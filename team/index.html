Jad Haddad: engineering projects

Vyrex Wrist Wearable

College Station, TX · Feb. 2026 to May 2026 · Originator, supply-side engineering, PCB design v3.0.0, compliance-conscious design, prior-art and patent research

Originated the concept for and led the technical design and R&D on an internal wrist-worn fall-detection wearable program. Directed the hardware architecture: a PCB design carried through Version 3.0.0, a bench prototype built on an ESP32 development board with an Adafruit LSM6DSOXTR six-axis IMU breakout, and a complete component specification covering the BLE MCU, inertial sensing, lithium-polymer charge management, USB-C power input, regulation, and MOSFET-driven haptic feedback with flyback protection. Led the IMU migration from the STM LSM6DSOXTR to the LSM6DSV16XTR for lower power draw, stronger on-chip machine-learning inference, and higher onboard storage, which reduced how often the device had to offload data and interrupt the MCU's deep-sleep state. Also led the MCU migration from a prototyping ESP32 devkit to an nRF52805 BLE 5.4 module before ultimately settling on an ESP32-C3 Mini, weighing assembler stock availability, power consumption, and pin compatibility. That final choice also avoided FCC 47 CFR Part 15C intentional-radiator testing, saving thousands of dollars. Also developed a companion app with a custom machine-learning algorithm: the device offloaded stored user gait data to the app, which retrained the model and adjusted the user's fall-detection parameters, then sent the updated parameters back to the device over Bluetooth into a fully custom, power-efficient deep-sleep interrupt system. Researched prior art and competitive patents in the fall-detection wearable space to inform the program's IP strategy. Vyrex was an internal research program and was ultimately not commercialized.

Capacitor Banks: The Fundamentals of Theory and Application

Oncor Electric Delivery · Glen Rose, TX · May 2026 to Aug. 2026

Researched capacitor bank theory and fault-response behavior for high-voltage transmission systems tied to the Comanche Peak Nuclear Power Plant. Covered series vs. parallel bank configurations, tap-sensing & unbalance protection, and relay-based fault response. Built a set of interactive animations from scratch in HTML/SVG to explain the concepts, including a phase-correction fault-response diagram, a sensing-circuit walkthrough, a series/parallel configuration comparison, and a power-triangle explainer using a beer-pour analogy for real vs. reactive power. This later became part of the team's new standard for explaining capacitor-bank behavior, enhancing on prior approaches to onboarding and training on the subject.

Smart Table Solutions

McKinney, TX · May 2025 to Jul. 2025 · STM32, Solid Edge, Multisim

Designed and sold an automated height-adjustable desk-control system built around an STM32 microcontroller. Led the hardware lifecycle in KiCad, including modular PCB schematics and layouts, motor-control circuitry, power management, and expansion interfaces. Modeled circuit behavior in Multisim, validated component selections, programmed the firmware, and completed enclosure modeling and integration.

SoilSense

McKinney, TX · Apr. 2025 to Jul. 2025 · C++, Arduino, sensor-based systems

Integrated capacitive soil-moisture monitoring, a passive water-safety shutoff, two-channel MOSFET switching, and ultrasonic sensors to track fluid levels.
